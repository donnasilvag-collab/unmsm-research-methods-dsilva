# Research protocol (v1.0)

## Release status

This is the current controlled release of the research protocol for the UNMSM course repository. `protocol_v0.1.md` is retained as the full earlier draft and source record. Version 1.0 confirms the study boundaries that must remain fixed before protected fieldwork begins.

## Study title

*Influence of information security risk management on the effectiveness of access control, source code protection, and software development traceability in Peruvian companies: protocol for a mixed methods study.*

## Research question and objective

The study asks how and to what extent the maturity of information security risk management influences the effectiveness of access control, source code protection, and software development traceability in Peruvian companies. Its objective is to analyze that relationship and explain the organizational, human, and operational factors associated with the observed results.

## Design and scope

The study uses an explanatory sequential mixed methods design with a pragmatic orientation. The quantitative phase will examine associations between risk-management maturity and the three control dimensions. The qualitative phase will use semistructured interviews and authorized document review to explain patterns that require contextual interpretation.

The planned quantitative sample is approximately 50 to 80 participants from 6 to 10 Peruvian software-development organizations. The qualitative phase will involve 10 to 15 voluntary key informants selected from the quantitative results. Recruitment, consent, storage, and disclosure controls are specified in `09_ethics/` and `10_data_mgmt/`.

## Data boundaries

The study will not request proprietary source code, passwords, access tokens, private repository addresses, security configurations, vulnerability details, or incident evidence. Public repository information in `05_pipeline/` is a separate descriptive benchmark. It does not replace fieldwork, measure internal security maturity, or support causal or organization-level claims.

## Analysis and reporting

The quantitative phase will use descriptive analysis, instrument-consistency review, and association analysis appropriate to the collected data. The qualitative phase will use thematic analysis. Meta-inferences will integrate the two phases while distinguishing observed results from interpretation.

Public reporting will use aggregated and disclosure-reviewed outputs. The repository may include code, instruments, documentation, and approved derived results, but it will not contain raw survey responses, recordings, unredacted transcripts, contact information, confidential organizational documents, or security-sensitive material.
