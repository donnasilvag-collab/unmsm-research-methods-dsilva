01 · Research overview
Concise research summary

The study examines how risk management maturity is associated with secure development controls

This doctoral protocol examines access control, source code protection, and development traceability in Peruvian software companies. An explanatory sequential mixed methods design will use survey patterns to guide interviews and authorized document review. Fieldwork has not begun, so the current evidence is a reproducible public benchmark rather than a causal test.
Research projectOperational security in Peruvian software development

The study focuses on companies with an identifiable software function and agile, DevOps, or continuous integration practices.

ApproachQuantitative patterns followed by contextual explanation

A survey establishes patterns. Interviews and authorized records then explain how those patterns arise in practice.

Current evidenceProtocol, literature review, and public benchmark

The repository documents the full design and a 48-repository demonstration. Company field data remain pending.

The study combines measurement, contextual explanation, and open documentation02

02 · Research problem
Starting point

The project addresses the gap between security policy and operational control

Fast delivery in agile and DevOps settings can create a gap between written policy and actual decisions about access, repositories, and software changes.

Having tools is not enough. The study examines whether greater risk-management maturity is associated with controls that remain effective and verifiable over time.
01Unauthorized access: poorly governed privileges can affect systems and development assets.
02Code exposure: repositories and artifacts contain intellectual property and operational secrets.
03Incomplete traceability: changes without enough evidence hinder audit, attribution, and response.
04Evidence gap: integrated research on these relationships is scarce in Peruvian companies.
The study examines operational effectiveness rather than the mere presence of policies or tools03

05 · Method selection
Pragmatic paradigm

An explanatory sequential design best fits the research question

Selected option Explanatory sequential mixed methods

The quantitative phase estimates patterns between variables. The qualitative phase examines why those patterns appear, weaken, or conflict in practice.

A quantitative study alone would rely heavily on self-report. A case study would provide context but offer less comparative capacity.

08 · Ethics and data management
Foreseeable risks and safeguards

Safeguards begin before any data are collected

Employment or reputational harmA statement may expose a weak practice

Pseudonymization, aggregated results, and removal of identifiable quotations reduce this risk.

Security exposureAn interview may reveal sensitive material

The study will not request credentials, proprietary code, configurations, vulnerabilities, or incident details.

Hierarchical pressureOrganizational access may influence participation

Direct consent, withdrawal without penalty, and no individual lists for employers protect participants.

ReidentificationContext can identify people without naming them

Broad categories, separated identifiers, and review before publication limit disclosure.

Public dataBenchmark data, scripts, parameters, and approved aggregate results.
Restricted dataSurveys, recordings, transcripts, contacts, and authorized documents stored outside GitHub.
Data not requestedCredentials, proprietary source code, secrets, vulnerabilities, and incident details.

Dependence

The 24 repositories in the Peru stratum belong to nine owners. Clustered intervals are wider in all four dimensions.

Coverage limits

The sample is purposive, internal signals are not visible, and OpenSSF scores are available for 19 of the 48 records.

Integrity

AI use is disclosed, the student verifies each decision, and retracted references are excluded using documented evidence.

The benchmark is neither a predictive model nor an assessment of demographic fairness13

14 · Conclusions and next steps
Conclusion and future improvements

The protocol is coherent, but the doctoral claim still depends on field evidence

Revalidate the systematic review and confirm the publication status of every reference.
Validate the questionnaire, interview guide, and operational definitions.
Complete ethical review and formalize access to participating organizations.
Run and integrate both phases without treating public signals as internal controls.

Expected contribution: explain which organizational conditions are associated with more consistent security controls during software development.
