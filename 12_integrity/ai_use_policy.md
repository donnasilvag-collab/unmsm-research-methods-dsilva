# AI use policy for this research repository

## Scope and responsibility

This policy applies to generative AI and other automated assistance used in the course deliverables, protocol, literature review, code, analysis, presentation materials, and future thesis documents associated with *Madurez de la gestión de riesgos y eficacia de los controles de desarrollo seguro en empresas peruanas*.

Donna Silva is responsible for every claim, citation, method, decision, result, and published file. An AI output has no evidentiary status merely because it is fluent or technically plausible. It enters the research record only after the author verifies it against the relevant source, data, or executable result.

## Permitted uses

The following uses are permitted with ordinary author review and a concise disclosure when they materially affect a document:

- language correction, translation, and readability editing of non-sensitive text;
- suggestions for headings, tables, file organization, or presentation structure;
- explanation of code or software commands using public or synthetic examples;
- formatting references after the author has verified each bibliographic record;
- generation of test cases that contain no participant or confidential company data;
- consistency checks across titles, research questions, objectives, filenames, and documented parameters;
- accessibility support, including alternative wording and descriptions of public figures or diagrams.

These uses may change expression and organization. They may not change the author's substantive decision without review.

## Conditional uses

The following uses require a recorded purpose, source verification, and explicit author approval before the output is retained:

| Use | Conditions |
| --- | --- |
| Drafting a substantive paragraph | Compare every claim with cited or primary material; rewrite unsupported or generic language; disclose material assistance. |
| Literature discovery or synthesis | Open the source; verify title, authors, DOI, publication status, method, and finding; record screening decisions independently. |
| Code generation or modification | Review the code line by line; run compilation, data validation, reproducibility, and sensitivity checks; do not treat successful execution as proof of methodological validity. |
| Quantitative analysis suggestions | Decide the estimand and assumptions before using the suggestion; test the implementation with synthetic or approved data; retain the author's analysis rationale. |
| Qualitative coding support | Obtain ethics and data-processing approval first; remove identifying and sensitive detail; keep the human coding decision and audit trail; never allow the tool to make the final interpretation. |
| Instrument drafting | Trace each item to a construct and source; complete expert review, cognitive testing, pilot testing, and ethics review before administration. |
| Translation of participant material | Use only an approved secure environment and verify meaning against the source; do not send protected transcripts to an external service without authorization. |
| Creation of figures or slides | Check every number, label, citation, and limitation against the repository; avoid invented results or visual certainty not supported by the analysis. |

OpenAI Codex may assist with repository editing and executable checks under these conditions. Naming one tool in this policy does not authorize it to receive protected data.

## Prohibited uses

AI tools must not be used to:

- fabricate or complete missing citations, DOI values, quotations, data, participants, search records, exclusion decisions, or results;
- submit raw survey responses, recordings, transcripts, contact details, consent records, credentials, proprietary source code, private repository addresses, confidential documents, vulnerability details, security configurations, or incident records to an unapproved external service;
- infer protected or sensitive attributes that the study did not collect;
- impersonate a participant, reviewer, adviser, ethics committee, or coauthor;
- make the final decision on consent, eligibility, withdrawal, risk acceptance, literature inclusion, qualitative coding, authorship, or interpretation;
- score, rank, or profile a person or company for an operational decision;
- conceal AI assistance when course, university, journal, funder, or publisher rules require disclosure;
- cite an AI response as evidence for a factual or scientific claim when a primary source is available;
- paraphrase a source in a way that hides plagiarism or exceeds permitted use of copyrighted material;
- generate a conclusion that is not reproducible from the approved data and documented analysis.

## Information classification rule

Before using an AI tool, classify the intended input:

| Class | Examples | AI handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Published articles, public repository files, synthetic examples, public standards | Permitted subject to source and license checks |
| Internal working material | Unpublished drafts without participant or company information | Conditional; minimize the content and check tool terms and project rules |
| Protected research data | Survey responses, transcripts, consent records, recruitment logs, organization codes, controlled documentary evidence | Prohibited in external AI services unless the ethics approval, consent, contract, and data-processing assessment explicitly authorize the use |
| Security-sensitive or secret | Credentials, proprietary code, exploit details, private configuration, customer data, incident evidence | Prohibited |

When classification is uncertain, treat the material as protected and do not submit it.

## Verification procedure

For any retained AI-assisted contribution, the author will perform the checks that apply:

1. Identify the purpose of the assistance and the input class.
2. Compare factual claims with the cited source or authoritative record.
3. Verify every citation, DOI, author list, year, and publication status.
4. Run generated or modified code in the documented environment and inspect its outputs.
5. Check whether the output changes the protocol, an eligibility decision, a score, or an interpretation. If it does, record the human decision and reason.
6. Remove promotional language, vague authority, unsupported certainty, repetitive structure, and other wording that obscures the evidence.
7. Record material use in the document disclosure, commit history, or protected research log as appropriate.

The author will correct the public repository promptly if an AI-assisted error is found. A correction will identify the affected file, the evidence used, and whether any dependent result changed.

## Minimum AI-use record

Material assistance should be recorded with enough detail to reconstruct the author's review without preserving sensitive prompts.

| Field | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Date | Date of use |
| Tool and version, when available | Product name and model or service version |
| Purpose | Editing, code, literature support, figure, or other specific task |
| Input classification | Public, internal, protected, or security-sensitive |
| Output retained | File, section, or code component affected |
| Human verification | Source check, test, comparison, or decision performed |
| Author decision | Accepted, revised, or rejected, with a short reason |

Prompts containing credentials or protected data must not be preserved in this record. The record itself follows the data-management plan.

## Thesis-ready declaration

The following declaration may be used in the thesis after checking that it still describes the completed work:

> Generative AI tools, including OpenAI Codex, were used during preparation of this research for language editing, document organization, code support, and consistency checks. They did not determine the research question, participant eligibility, literature-inclusion decisions, interpretation of evidence, or final conclusions. I verified bibliographic records against their sources, reviewed AI-assisted text, and tested executable outputs in the documented environment. I did not submit participant data, confidential organizational material, credentials, proprietary source code, or security-sensitive records to an external AI service. I, Donna Silva, accept responsibility for the accuracy, integrity, and originality of the thesis.

If later use differs from this statement, the declaration must be revised rather than retained for convenience.
